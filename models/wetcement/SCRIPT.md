Ink: black toner on white office paper, nothing else.

## Page 1
Words:
- UNPROMPTED (headline, marker hand-lettering)

Picture: Cover. One figure stands dead center on an empty white page, drawn in thick marker, simplified: too-big jacket, flat hair, blank face, no mouth line. The figure holds a staple gun down at one side and a stack of flyers so tall it reaches the chin, one arm hooked under it. The stack is drawn as a slab of hand-cut paper layers, each layer a real cut edge with a small shadow. The title UNPROMPTED is hand-lettered in fat marker caps across the top, letters uneven, the P slightly lower than the rest. Nothing else on the page. A dark toner band runs down the right edge and clips the last letter.

## Page 2
Words:
- WET CEMENT (headline)
- FIRST SHOW EVER (headline)
- SAT THE 4TH (small text)
- THE ANNEX (small text)

Picture: The flyer, full page, held up flat toward the reader by two drawn hands that enter from the bottom edge. The flyer is a hand-cut rectangle taped at all four corners, its cut edges casting shadows. WET CEMENT is marker lettering, huge, filling the top third. FIRST SHOW EVER sits under it in smaller marker. Below that, two typewriter strips taped down slightly crooked: SAT THE 4TH, then THE ANNEX. Center of the flyer is a halftone square cut from a copied newspaper photo: a crowd shot, dots coarse and blown out, nobody's face readable. White paper all around the flyer. No figure except the hands.

## Page 3
Words: none

Picture: Wide empty street, drawn with two lines: a sidewalk edge and a curb. One telephone pole stands off to the left, thick marker, one bolt, one staple line. The figure stands at the pole in profile, arm up, staple gun pressed to a single flyer, face turned to the flyer, blank. The flyer is a small hand-cut rectangle, its printing too small to read, one corner already lifting. The rest of the page is bare white paper. A halftone strip of sky, cut from a newspaper, runs along the very top like a horizon band, ending in a ragged tear.

## Page 4
Words:
- SAT THE 4TH (small text, struck through in marker)
- THE 11TH (small text, marker hand-lettering)

Picture: Close on the pole, waist height, cropped so the pole fills the left half and the figure is only an elbow and a hand at the right edge. One flyer, same hand-cut rectangle, now weathered: a corner torn, two staples doubled. The typewriter strip SAT THE 4TH has a thick marker line drawn straight through it. Under the strike, written directly on the flyer in marker, larger and rougher than the typing: THE 11TH. The halftone crowd square is still there, now with a staple through one of the faces. Generous white everywhere else.

Picture: Extreme close crop on the bottom of the flyer, no figure at all, the paper filling the whole page so the reader is reading over someone's shoulder. ## Page 6
Words:
- THE SONGS ARE ALMOST DONE (small text, typewriter strip)

Picture: The practice room, seen square on, nearly empty. One amp sits on the floor, still in its cardboard box, box flaps open, the amp untouched inside; the box is a collaged photocopy of a real carton with its printing reversed out. No drums, no mic stand, no other person. The figure sits on the floor beside the box, knees up, staple gun resting on one knee, the flyer stack beside them, face blank and aimed straight at the reader. One typewriter strip is taped along the bottom edge of the page, off center: THE SONGS ARE ALMOST DONE. The room is otherwise white paper, one line for the floor.

## Page 7
Words:
- FOR LEASE (headline, collaged sign lettering)

Picture: The front of the venue, flat and head on, drawn in thick marker: a door with a board nailed across it diagonally, one window papered over from inside. A sign is collaged over the window, cut from a photocopied real estate board, its letters clean and typeset against everything else: FOR LEASE. The figure stands small at the bottom of the frame with their back to the reader, one flyer hanging from one hand, staple gun in the other, not raised. The door step is empty. A dark toner band runs across the bottom of the page and swallows the figure's feet.

## Page 8
Words:
- WET CEMENT (headline)
- FIRST SHOW EVER (headline)
- SOON (small text, marker hand-lettering)

Picture: The same telephone pole from page 3, now grown: the pole is a fat column of layered flyers, wider than the figure, hundreds of hand-cut paper edges stacked and shadowed all the way up, staples glinting as small white gaps. It runs off the top of the page. The figure stands on tiptoe at the far right of the column, arm stretched as high as it goes, pressing one fresh flyer to the very top of what they can reach, face blank. The fresh flyer is the only legible one: WET CEMENT, FIRST SHOW EVER, and under it, where the date strip used to be, one marker word: SOON. No date. Everything else is white paper.
