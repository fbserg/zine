Ink: blue

## Page 1
Words: UNPROMPTED (headline)

Picture: Square cover, mostly empty off-white paper. The title runs across the top third in bold cut-paper black capitals, one line, tight letters. Below and slightly left of centre stands Pim, the whole book's character: a small rubbery figure in a wool toque and a sweater two sizes too big, thick even black outline, two dot eyes, no mouth, front on, arms down, a stubby pencil held loose in the right hand. Pim is drawn in black only. Behind Pim and to the right, rising past the top edge of the frame and partly behind the title, is a tall flat rectangle with one horizontal slot at chest height: the machine. The machine is flat blue with no black line anywhere on it, so the 2 mm misregistration makes it sit a little out of phase with everything else. No feet, no face, no logo. Pim's expression is blank. Bottom half of the page is bare paper.

## Page 2
Words: It eats drawings. (small text)

Picture: A flat wide view of a basement apartment, drawn as simple black lines: one long window strip up near the ceiling, a radiator under it, bare floor. At the far left, Pim sits in profile at a small drawing table, pencil to paper, knees up, blank dot eyes, hunched a little. At the far right, floor to ceiling, stands the machine: a plain flat blue rectangle with one black-free slot at chest height and a thin blue cord looping down to a wall outlet. Between them, a wide stretch of empty floor and empty paper. Everything Pim owns is black outline. Everything the machine is, is blue. The small typewriter line sits low in the left margin, under the table.

## Page 3
Words: I feed it mine. (small text)

Picture: A close crop, most of the frame bare paper. From the left edge, Pim's two rubbery black hands enter holding a single small sheet with a bird drawn on it in black: a plain pigeon, four lines, one dot eye, clearly hand drawn and clearly not good. The hands are sliding the sheet into the machine's slot, which enters from the right edge as a flat blue horizontal bar and nothing else. The blue slot overlaps the black sheet slightly where the ink misses register. In the top left corner, small, Pim's head is tipped into frame watching the sheet go in, dot eyes level, no mouth. Nothing else on the page.

## Page 4
Words: It gives back forty. (small text)

Picture: The machine head on, centred, a tall flat blue rectangle with its slot now wide open. Out of the slot bursts a fan of identical blue sheets, dozens of them, arcing up and falling to pile across the whole bottom of the frame. Every sheet carries the same pigeon, but each one is wrong in its own way: six legs, beak on backwards, two heads, wings on upside down, one with the eye outside the head, all drawn flat and blue and confident. The pile buries the lower third of the page. At the far left edge, small and upright and mostly cut off by the frame, stands Pim in black, arms at sides, dot eyes flat, watching. Pim is not running.

## Page 5
Words: None of them are mine. (small text)

Picture: A high angle looking straight down at the apartment floor. The floor is paved corner to corner with the blue sheets, overlapping like fallen leaves, every one a wrong pigeon. Pim sits cross legged in the middle of them, drawn small and from above in black outline only, holding one blue sheet up close to the dot eyes with both hands. The toque is the top of the head. There is no black drawing anywhere on the floor, nowhere, only blue. Pim's shoulders are down. No mouth, no tears, no motion lines. The one word line sits in the bottom right corner where the blue sheets thin out and a little bare paper shows.

## Page 6
Words: So I stopped. (small text)

Picture: A wide flat view, almost all empty paper, the two figures pushed to opposite edges. At the far left, Pim sits on a plain kitchen chair facing out of the frame, away from the machine, hands empty and open in the lap, shoulders round, dot eyes blank. The stubby pencil lies on the floor beside the chair, black, still. At the far right stands the machine, flat blue, and its slot has changed: it hangs open wide and droops at the corners, tilted toward Pim, the exact shape of a baby bird's mouth waiting to be fed. The whole middle of the page is bare floor and bare paper. The distance between them is the joke. Nothing else in the frame.

## Page 7
Words: It got thin. (small text)

Picture: A street, flat side view. Along the bottom, a snowbank in flat blue, salt stained at the edge, with two black streetcar rails cutting across the road above it and a black hydro pole at the right holding a strip of blue overhead wire. Pim walks left to right in the middle of the frame, hands in pockets, toque on, black outline, dot eyes forward, blank. Following a few steps behind is the machine, now much narrower and sagging in the middle like an empty bag, bent forward at the top toward Pim, its blue cord dragging loose through the snow behind it. The slot is still open. Sky is bare paper. Nobody else is on the street.

## Page 8
Words: (none)

Picture: A far, still view, the widest page in the book. On the right, Pim sits on the concrete front steps of a narrow bay and gable house, knees up, a small black notebook open on the knees, one hand cupped over the page to hide it, shoulder turned away from the street, head down, drawing. Pim is small in the frame and entirely black outline. On the left edge, across a bare white road with two black rails in it, the thin blue machine stands behind a blue snowbank, upright, slot shut flat, facing Pim. Between them is a wide field of empty paper. No words anywhere on the page.
