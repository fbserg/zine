Ink: fluorescent orange

## Page 1
Words: Headline: UNPROMPTED. Small text: MARTA SIGNS (painted on a leaning plank at the foot of the ladder).
Picture: Square frame, a wooden A-frame ladder stands against a bare brick wall. Marta is halfway up it in a paint-stained coverall, one hand steadying the top rail, the other holding a wide flat brush loaded with orange paint above a blank rectangle marked with faint pencil guidelines. Her paint bucket, brush handle, and coverall pocket are orange; everything else, including the brick, the ladder, and her plain round dot-eyed face, stays black line on bare paper. A leaning plank sign at the ladder's foot reads MARTA SIGNS in her own uneven hand lettering. A drop cloth pools under the ladder's feet. Her expression is blank and pleased.

## Page 2
Words: (none)
Picture: The same wall, but a finished sign now hangs above a bakery doorway, its face turned edge-on to the reader so no letters show, only its orange-painted trim catching light along the border. Marta stands below on the sidewalk wiping her hands on a rag while a customer in an apron shakes her other hand, both faces blank and content. Her ladder is folded flat and leaning against the wall behind her. A paper bag of buns sits on the step. Thin black streetcar wires cross the sky overhead.

## Page 3
Words: Small text: NEW (on a paper banner taped across the storefront next door).
Picture: The storefront directly across the street, empty in earlier pages, now holds a flat black rectangular panel where a sign would go, its face crawling with tiny identical black glyphs cycling in rows too small to read. A small black lens juts from the panel's upper frame, angled across the street toward Marta's wall. A crooked paper banner reading NEW is taped below it, lettered only in black marker, no orange anywhere on this storefront. Marta is a small figure in the distance on her own sidewalk, ladder under one arm, head turned toward the panel.

## Page 4
Words: (none)
Picture: A split square, divided down the middle. The left half is a close crop of Marta's own hand-lettered bakery sign, now shown face-on: its capital M is built from two uneven brushed strokes with a looping orange-tipped serif where the brush lifted. The right half shows the same M rendered on the black panel from the previous page, identical loop and lean but built from flat, dead-straight black lines with no texture and no orange, printed rather than painted. The two Ms are lined up at the frame's centre seam, almost touching.

## Page 5
Words: (none)
Picture: Close on Marta's face and one raised hand, her finger resting on the brushed M of her own sign. Her dot eyes are shifted sideways toward the black panel's faint glow just off-frame, her mouth a flat closed line, the rest of her expression unchanged. A smear of orange paint sits on her fingertip where it touches the letter. Everything behind her fades to bare paper.

## Page 6
Words: Speech bubble, small text: THAT'S MY M. Second speech bubble, small text: IT'S THE FONT.
Picture: Inside the new shop, a young owner behind a counter shrugs with both palms up, the black panel glowing on the wall behind them, its rows of tiny glyphs still cycling. Marta stands at the counter pointing back over her shoulder toward the street, a speech bubble near her head reading THAT'S MY M. The owner's reply, IT'S THE FONT, sits in a bubble near theirs. The owner and counter are plain black line work; only Marta's coverall and paint-stained knuckles carry any orange in the room.

## Page 7
Words: (none)
Picture: Evening. Marta stands alone inside her own dark shopfront, a brush hanging loose from one lowered hand, dried orange paint on its bristles. She stares out the window at the black panel's cold glow, reflected as a small pale rectangle over her own tired face in the glass. Behind her reflection a streetcar slides past, reduced to a row of small black windows. Her dot eyes are open but flat, her shoulders down.

## Page 8
Words: Small text on a new plank sign, hand lettered: STILL HERE.
Picture: Morning. Marta is back on her ladder at her own shopfront, hanging a new plank sign reading STILL HERE, its letters visibly uneven, one orange serif dripped into a small tail she has not bothered to fix. Across the street in the background, the black panel's face has collapsed into broken static, its rows of glyphs smeared into a scribble trying and failing to loop the same drip, no orange anywhere on it. Marta does not look back at it. Her expression is still blank, her brush already loaded for the next letter.
