UNPROMPTED, an eight-page mini zine, riso printed: black ink plus one second ink you choose from the standard drums: fluorescent pink, fluorescent orange, blue, green, or metallic gold. Flat shapes, deadpan.
One recurring character carries the whole thing, page to page, like a small comic. The joke sits in the drawing. Word budget for the whole book, cover included: sixty. Eight pages, no more, no fewer.
No edition number, no price, no colophon ceremony.
It should read as ingroup to people who make and buy these things, without ever performing it. No winking. The tell is what you leave out.
Made in Toronto. Light touch: a detail or two the reader can spot, never announced.
This issue, the reference: a twelve-panel strip. A small brown-ink figure on the end of a diving board over an empty concrete pool, low hills behind. Panels 1 and 5 say "i love doing anything all the time" and "i like what ever happens" in a wobbly hand while the figure flips, hangs, and falls in the silent panels between. The last row: the figure climbs back up and sits on the board, and the final three panels say "you have to practice lying to yourself" "and feeling uncomfortable" "or you'll never be any good at it". Take the mood and the method, not the panels: cheerful sentences over a body doing something that is not cheerful. Your own figure, your own place, your own words.
Everything else is yours: the story, the words, what is drawn, how each page is laid out.

Notes after the first print:
- A reader has to get the story on one pass, cold. If a page needs the previous page explained, the page is wrong.
- Do not fill space. Most pages want one figure, one thing, and paper. A wall of stuff is a page that lost its nerve.
- Skip anything that only exists because zines usually have it.

State your ink choice on the first line of the script as: Ink: <colour>.
