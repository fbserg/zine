Ink: fluorescent orange

Rule for the whole book: the man and his world print in black line. The machine, and everything the machine makes or says, prints in flat fluorescent orange, misregistered 2 mm up and to the right. Print anything he cannot read as an orange squiggle, never as letters.

## Page 1
Words: UNPROMPTED (headline)

Picture: Cover. A square of bare off-white paper. Dead centre, low, one rubbery man in a slumped armchair, seen straight on, drawn in thick even black outline, legs out, hands flat on his thighs, small dot eyes, mouth a small calm curve. A squat orange box with two short arms stands on his chest, no bigger than a toaster, flat orange shape with a black outline only where it overlaps him, holding an open black-outlined book up in front of his face. He is not looking at the book. He is looking out at the reader. The title UNPROMPTED runs across the top third in bold black cut-paper capitals, wide, touching both edges of the page. Nothing else on the page, no border, no chair shadow.

## Page 2
Words: huh (small text)

Picture: Bare page, high horizon of nothing. A cereal box stands alone on the left, black outline, and the man leans in from the right at the waist, hands behind his back, nose almost touching it, dot eyes, flat mouth. Every line of print on the box is a single continuous orange squiggle, four squiggle lines where the words should be, sliding 2 mm off the black panel that holds them. The small typewriter word huh sits just above his head, black, tiny, no speech bubble, no tail. Big empty paper below both figures.

## Page 3
Words: FREE TOY INSIDE (headline)

Picture: Same bare room, camera pulled back. The orange box machine sits on the floor at the left, small, holding the cereal box up with both stub arms. Out of the machine, filling the whole upper right of the page, the words FREE TOY INSIDE in bold orange cut-paper capitals, stacked in three lines, no bubble, no tail, just the letters in the air, misregistered off their thin black keyline. The man stands at the right edge, tiny by comparison, both arms straight up, feet apart, mouth a wide black oval. He is facing the machine, not the words.

## Page 4
Words: he is better at it (small text)

Picture: One figure group, centred, acres of paper around it. The orange machine sits cross-legged on the floor with a small black-outlined plastic toy, a little windup dog, working it with both arms. The man lies on his front beside it, chin propped on both hands, elbows on the floor, knees bent with his feet in the air, watching the toy with dot eyes and a soft closed smile. The toy itself is black; the orange machine overlaps it so the orange slides across the dog's back. Small typewriter text he is better at it in black, bottom left corner, low and quiet.

## Page 5
Words: close (small text)

Picture: Close crop, waist up, the man at a bare table, seen from the front. He grips a black pen in his fist like a child, tongue out a fraction, brows level, dot eyes down. On the white form in front of him, where a signature belongs, he has drawn a small careful house with a door and a window and a curl of chimney smoke, black line. The form's printed lines are orange squiggles. No machine on this page. The word close, small typewriter black, sits directly under the house like a caption.

## Page 6
Words: perfect (small text)

Picture: Same crop, same table, same page of paper, but the man has leaned back out of the way so only his black-outlined arm and shoulder show at the right edge. The orange machine stands on the table, both arms on one pen, laying down a long looping orange signature that runs off the edge of the form and 2 mm off the black ruled line beneath it. The little house is still there beside it, black, untouched. The man's one visible hand gives a small flat thumbs up. The word perfect, small typewriter black, bottom right.

## Page 7
Words: none

Picture: Outdoors, or the suggestion of it: a single black park bench on bare paper, no trees, no ground line. The man sits on the bench facing us, ankles crossed, both eyes dots, mouth a small curve, looking off to the left at nothing. The orange machine stands on the bench beside him, holding his right wrist up with both arms and swinging it in a wave at someone off the right edge of the page. His waving hand is limp. He is not looking at whoever he is waving at. A faint second orange outline of the waving arm shows the swing.

## Page 8
Words: YOU ARE HAVING A GREAT TIME (headline)

Picture: Centre of the page, the man alone in a deck chair, black outline, arms flopped over the sides, bare feet, head tipped back a little, dot eyes wide open, mouth stretched into a huge black grin. He is looking straight out at the reader. The orange machine stands at the right on a stack of nothing, holding up a large flat orange card that fills the right half of the page, angled toward the man's face and away from us, printed with YOU ARE HAVING A GREAT TIME in orange cut-paper capitals, six words, four lines, misregistered off the card's thin black keyline. His eyes are not on the card. Nothing else on the page.
