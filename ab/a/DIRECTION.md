UNPROMPTED, issue 002. An eight-page anti-AI mini zine, riso printed: black ink plus fluorescent pink, flat shapes, deadpan.
One recurring character carries the whole thing, page to page, like a small comic. Weird and sad before angry. The joke sits in the drawing; words stay few and dry.
The zine claims human authorship and never hints otherwise. No edition number, no price, no colophon ceremony.
Made in Toronto. Light touch: a detail or two the reader can spot, never announced.
It should read as ingroup to people who make and buy these things, without ever performing it. No winking, no "look, I am one of you". The tell is what you leave out.
Never use the words artificial, intelligence, model, LLM, chatbot, or prompt anywhere.
The machine has to be visible inside the book, not only in the title. A stranger who never saw the cover should still know what the character is up against.
Everything else is yours: the story, the words, what is drawn, how each page is laid out.

Notes after the first print:
- A reader has to get the story on one pass, cold. If a page needs the previous page explained, the page is wrong.
- Do not fill space. Most pages want one figure, one thing, and paper. A wall of stuff is a page that lost its nerve.
- Skip anything that only exists because zines usually have it.
